A few weeks back, I had connected with a fellow leader and the topic of preparing for and trying to prevent burnout in the workplace came up. As many companies are asking teams to "do more with less" (I cringed writing that) coupled with the almost hourly changes in technology, never ending demands and various deadlines, burnout is almost always an afterthought.

Now these field notes and tips I compiled are nowhere near a magic solution to burnout, but they have seemed to work quite well for myself as well as for individuals I have had the chance to work with.

One of the first things to consider from a high level about burnout is actually the sheer amount of time you may be spending at work or just in work mode. A 40 hour work week is roughly 24% of your available time. I know some real heavy hitting math I did to come up with that figure. But in all seriousness and in many cases employees are working more hours than that. Whether it's mentally planning for a big upcoming presentation, knocking out a few weekend or late night emails, thinking about how you are going to deliver feedback, decompressing after a tough call or something similar, there are dozens of unaccounted hours in a week spent processing and cycling through work related items.

I am not a specialist on human psychology or behavior but both mentally and physically being plugged into work activities for 24% to more realistically 27% to 29% ish of your week is substantial. Do that for 50+ weeks non-stop and you are going to be fried.

With all of that in mind, here are my go to's for finding your rhythm, planning to recharge and trying to integrate habits to try and curtail burnout before it fully sets in.

## Mental Pacing & Stamina

Everyone has varying levels of mental stamina. Some are olympic level sprinters, some are marathoners, some are triathlon competitors and the list could go on as each stamina category has its own advantages. I personally resonate with something along the lines of long-distance walking (believe the Aussies call it bushwalking) for how I operate where it's not too fast, not too slow, just trying to cover a bit of ground each day so I can operate steadily over a few months. Now to recharge my mental stamina, a few days off or an extended Holiday weekend helps me from burning out. For someone who may be more of a sprinter (think of that YouTuber, Mr. Beast), where they can rip through 2 to 3 weeks of work full tilt, no days off then followed by a handful of days off does wonders for them. The key takeaway from this is asking yourself, "What pacing and stamina align with me so I can run my own race and be the best version of me?"

## "Failing to prepare is preparing to fail"

"Failing to prepare is preparing to fail" - Ben Franklin or John Wooden?… they both liked to be prepared.

  Now with your own mental pacing and stamina in mind, you have to turn that into action. As a leader one of the 1st things I do in early January is put together a month by month overview and run through it on a team call. It's imperative to encourage your team to be aware of company-wide days off, holidays, EOQ's, seasonal flows of your business as it enables you to drive team ownership but also instill positive association of "Hey, let's all plan for taking time off". Also don't forget to follow up with your team on this in your 1-2-1s following the team call!

  To take that one step further as a leader, showing your team the days you will be out of office is a game changer. This helps twofold: #1. You are leading by example. #2. You provide an opportunity for individuals who want to get some exposure and visibility into leadership tasks.

  As a whole from both a leadership and individual standpoint, being intentional via visualizing time off seems to alleviate coverage gaps, empower all to plan to recharge as well as give everyone a great jump on scoring some cheap flights too.

## Deliberate Habits

Breaking old habits and starting new habits is easier said than done… I mean, James Clear has sold over 25 million copies and counting of Atomic Habits yet we as humans all face the challenge of actually implementing change for the habits we aspire to possess. So with action in mind, one of the first habits is Focus Fridays where the 1st Friday of each month is designated to having no calls on calendars. In short, the value of Focus Fridays creates an opportunity for individuals to take several paths and all of them help curb burnout.

Some use Focus Fridays to dive deep into building, learning, upskilling or certifications which is awesome. Others leverage Focus Fridays for more change of scenery centric items, such as working from a local coffee shop, syncing up with others in the same or different industry or even taking a mid-morning / early-afternoon workout class to break up the monotony. The key item with Focus Fridays is, if you are going to have them, they have to be honored and respected by all on the team (no surprise calls). If they are not, this has inverse effects and accelerates burnout.

  Now similar to a physical change of scenery, another habit is physical reward. Given how much work is conducted virtually with non-tangible items, a physical reward or action is overlooked.

  As ridiculous as it sounds at EOQ, once all my reporting is done, I reward myself with a piece of Tennessee Waltz Cake…besides the taste of this phenomenal cake, this small physical reward is a dopamine marker that I use in conjunction with mental pacing and stamina. I am not saying you need to necessarily buy yourself anything, but what I am saying is enjoy the fruits of your labor in association with your work pace and milestones.
</SplitFigure>

Here we go, the last two habits for burnout. Hang with me here. First one being, knowing when you are burning too hot. If you find yourself obsessing over granular details, iterating multiple times on something that has low impact value, or spinning your wheels on something you can't control, you may be burning too hot and your productivity will feel it.

This last habit dovetails nicely with burning too hot and is pretty curt. When you are done with work you need to be done with work. Physically close your computer, don't look at your emails or messages, set up reduced notifications. Go do anything other than work. Building that habit into your daily routine has been blurred significantly with WFH but if you can master that even for a few days a week it'll help alleviate being stuck in work mode and burnout.
