## Good for Who?

Recent college/university-grads or really anyone under the age of 30 ish.

## When to Read?

This really should be part of college/university curricula for any business majors. Also ideal for anyone who finds themselves scratching their head wondering, “How are people affording that lifestyle?”.

## My 2¢

This is a book that should be read twice in your life. The first time ideally for individuals 18 to 21 years old or in their 2nd or 3rd year of university. The second time would be right around 27 to 30 ish years old. The logic being at each one of these stages of your life such as committing to an professional area of focus as well as working through several years of early career jobs are impactful in establishing a tangible comprehension of money coupled with real life experiences. 

Post reading this, I find myself applying these ideologies into practice and picking up on patterns that I previously did not notice. There are many of the tried and true ones, such as: plan to save 6 to 12 months of living expenses, Freedom (I can do whatever I want today) is the highest form of wealth, and not everyone is playing the same game so take financial advice with a grain of salt which are all helpful.  

However, I'll round off with an ideology which makes me wish I had read this book long ago. So 6 or so months ago I was at the barbershop having a conversation and the topic of clients and customers came up. Long story short, It's revealed that one of the customers of the shop is a billionaire. No fancy clothes, no watch, normal car, just a regular guy I’ve seen multiple times in the years I have been going. So getting to the point as Housel said, “Wealth is what you don't see” or as Lil Wayne said it better, “Real G's move in silence like lasagna”. 

Save your money. Let it work for you. Spend it on experiences vs. material items. 

## Steal This

Man in the Car Paradox. Next time you see a cool car (or ugly car depending upon your taste); ask yourself do you care more about the car or the driver of the car? More often than not, you have no idea who the hell is behind the wheel and an association between car and driver is never formulated defeating the whole point of someone buying a car for clout.
